A prefix is not a clock

02:30 — what the check can and cannot say

The design is this. A long conversation lives in two places: the transcript on this side, and a session held by a vendor's agent on the other. When the process restarts, the client would like to pick the vendor's session up again and send only the newest message, instead of replaying everything. To know that is safe it keeps a small record: a fingerprint of every message as of the last turn that finished. On restart it asks one question — is the record a prefix of the transcript? If the history was edited, compacted or reordered, the prefix breaks and the client starts fresh with the whole transcript. That part is sound.

But a prefix answers only "has this diverged?" It does not answer "is this current?" A record that stopped being written three turns ago is still a prefix. I ran the two production functions unchanged, with a record of 1 fingerprint against a transcript of 7: the decision was to resume the old session, and the prompt that would be sent was the fourth question alone. The 2 turns in between are on neither side of the wire, and every check is green.

An old record passes; the gap is countable from the same inputs READY 

	


	
Inside one running process this cannot happen: the live session and
	its fingerprint advance together, and a turn that fails drops the
	session. Across a restart the record is the only witness, and it is
	written only after a turn that finished cleanly. Every turn that reaches
	the transcript without advancing the record — a turn that died midway,
	a turn whose record failed to save, a one-shot turn on the same
	conversation — leaves a witness that still passes. One of the day's
	own decisions (if saving the record fails, let the turn continue) is
	such a door, and a repair planned beside it (write the record
	atomically) makes the older record more durable, not less.


	
The discriminator needs nothing new. The messages after the record's
	prefix must hold exactly one user turn; more means the record is behind,
	and the client should start fresh and say so. Whether a record matches
	and whether a record is up to date are two questions, and a system that
	resumes must ask both. Two smaller notes went with it: the table has a
	state called "unmeasured" that
	0 rows use while
	7
	rows say the word in prose where the test cannot count it; and the live
	measurements name vendor versions that nothing compares with the version
	the package pins.


	
Evidence: the decision and the prompt were measured here
	on the lane's unpushed commit by importing the two functions; the status
	counts were counted from the table; that the production path will build
	such a record is read from code, not run — production does not read the
	record yet. The comment is on the thread, at his word. His words and his
	agents' words are not carried here.
